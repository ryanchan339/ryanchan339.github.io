Make the full research process inspectable

A prediction score does not explain how a portfolio is built or whether a model adds useful information. I built a pipeline that connects market data, feature engineering, model evaluation, portfolio constraints, and paper-trading workflows.

From prices to a constrained portfolio

The pipeline turns daily market history into momentum, volatility, moving-average, RSI, and cross-sectional features. A gradient-boosted classifier estimates relative performance against SPY. Its ranking is blended with momentum before the top five stocks become portfolio targets.

Automation with a reviewable boundary

  • Repeatable data preparation. Python, Pandas, and NumPy generate the features used for training and portfolio ranking.
  • Explicit allocation rules. The workflow selects five stocks, rebalances every five trading days, caps each holding at 15%, and caps total exposure at 75%.
  • Separate planning from execution. A scheduled workflow refreshes data, trains the model, and generates recommendations and a dry-run order plan. Paper-order submission is an explicit step through Alpaca.
  • Keep the outputs inspectable. The dashboard reads committed reports so predictions, comparisons, and recorded activity can be reviewed together.

What the evaluation showed

The project compares backtests with momentum and low-volatility baselines, then evaluates performance across five walk-forward folds. The committed dashboard summary reports a mean ROC AUC of about 0.506 and average excess return of roughly −0.7%. Those results show the model’s limits even when a headline backtest looks stronger.

The dashboard data was generated September 11, 2026. Walk-forward and baseline reports can cover earlier periods than the headline backtest, so their returns should not be treated as directly comparable. Backtests, simulation, and paper-account results remain distinct.
